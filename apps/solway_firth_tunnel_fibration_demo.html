A director’s question: what does a consent delay change?

The Solway tunnel teams share consent, design and safety gates. If consents move, the programme must decide whether later gates also move. This demo applies that decision to the dates of named deliverables while retaining each team’s supplied offset and exemption.

Trace one effect: M2 consents +60 days → with forward delay on, M3 design freeze +60 days → the ground model’s due date stays 10 days before M3. Its version can still disagree with Civil’s alignment: a later date does not resolve an interface mismatch.

Fictional teaching data. These are coordination rules, not a design, construction plan or verified engineering result.

What this example establishes

Computed: date offsets, a declared chain of gate delays, and five interface predicates.

Supplied: gates, deliverables, versions, offsets and interface rules are toy inputs. Marking work “done” is a user assertion.

Unchecked: actual geometry, evidence quality, resources, costs, engineering feasibility and unlisted dependencies. Passing these checks does not establish a global plan or a categorical fibration.

Compared with a schedule: an ordinary dependency/rules model can produce these same results. Scheduling tools already support cross-project relationships, concurrent tasks, baselines and time-based float. This example adds an explicit view of gate anchors and declared interfaces; its categorical interpretation remains a hypothesis.
