The handover game · Foray 210

Get the units in.
Bring the access out.

You’re leading a tunnel fit-out. Two units need installing and accepting before the site can be handed over.

A possession is your agreed window to do the work. Choose the jobs, dispatch the crews and deal with a changing shift. Learn why having the equipment in place is only part of being finished.

About 5 minutes per mission. A fictional learning game using the same action rules as The work that must exist.

Your shift

The first handover

Read the site. Choose the work.

  1. Select a work card. Its crew briefing explains what changes and why it is ready or blocked.
  2. Dispatch the crew. Game time advances only while work is underway or you choose a delay. Pause the animation or advance one hour yourself.
  3. Answer the learning questions and respond to shift decisions. Questions teach; they never substitute for completing the work.
  4. Finish every requested installation and inspection, remove temporary access, and stay within this mission’s limits.

You can remove access too early, or spend too much. Undo and restart let you learn from the result. You do not need to follow the shortest route.

Take the idea further

What is left when the work is over?

A gantry and the installation it enables belong to different descriptions of the project. The finished units are products; setting up, installing and inspecting are processes. The records are evidence, and the gantry is temporary means.

The rules behind the game

Physical actions, access constraints and completion obligations come directly from the essay’s model. Each inspection represents a successful inspection and its record.

Costs, hours, shift incidents and quiz scenarios are fictional training assumptions added for the game. They are not engineering estimates. The units are deliberately generic; this is a two-unit fit-out, not a simulation of constructing the whole Solway tunnel.

You learn through choices and explanations. It does not infer new methods, certify safety or model inspection failures.
